A letter of credit is a commitment issued by a bank on behalf of a buyer.

The bank agrees to pay the seller if the seller presents the required documents and complies with the terms of the letter of credit.

Common documents may include a commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, insurance certificate, and certificate of origin.

Letters of credit are commonly used in international trade because they reduce payment risk between buyers and sellers who may not know each other.

An applicant is usually the buyer or importer.

The beneficiary is usually the seller or exporter.

The issuing bank opens the letter of credit at the request of the applicant.

A confirming bank may add its own payment undertaking to the letter of credit.

Documentary compliance is essential. Banks examine documents rather than the physical goods.
